A fifth of our five-letter answers end in E

We counted every letter of Letter Lock’s 809 answers. The numbers are lopsided in ways you can use.

Behind Ziggle · The Ziggle team · 4-minute read · 14 June 2026

In this article 5 sections

Letter Lock hides one of 809 five-letter words each day, and accepts 5,049 as guesses (about 6.2 times as many, so you can try odd words without wasting a guess on a word that cannot be the answer). Every answer is also a valid guess. We counted the letters.

  • 22.5%end in E
  • 17.3%begin with S
  • 11.4%have a double letter
  • 7letter Js in all 4,045

First letters, last letters

The rare ones

All 26 letters appear. The least common are J and X (7 each), then Q (12) and Z (14); even V shows up only 51 times. If you are still stuck in the late game with a Q or a J in play, you probably have a short list: Letter Lock’s coach will show how many answers were still in play after each guess.

The twist

92 answers (11.4%) have a double letter. Five different letters can never test for a repeat, so if you never guess a word with one, those 92 answers are the ones that will catch you out. When the clues fit, it is worth spending a guess on a word with a repeated letter.

The deeper read

That was the short version. What follows goes further, with each fact read in its source.

Do our 809 words agree with the dictionary?

Letter frequency is one of the oldest tools of codebreaking, and the usual published orderings are of two kinds. The first counts letters in running text, which gives “etaoinshrdlcumwfgypbvkjxqz”[2] (a version from Lewand’s Cryptological Mathematics). The second counts letters in the entries of a dictionary, ignoring how often each word is used, and the Concise Oxford Dictionary gives “earioTnslcudpmhgbfywkvxzjq”, which starts E, A, R, I, O. Which kind do our five-letter answers resemble?

Our top ten letters are exactly the Oxford dictionary’s top ten, in a different order, and share eight with running text. That is what you would expect of a list of words: each word is counted once, with no bonus for the many times a word like “the” is used. The top eight letters carry about 60% of all the letters in the 809 answers (computed); for English in general the Wikipedia article gives about 65% for the top eight and about 80% for the top twelve[2] (ours: 60% and 77%, so a list of five-letter words is spread a little more evenly than English as a whole).

The craft behind the counts

Counting letters is older than computers. The method of breaking a cipher by letter frequency is credited to the Arab scholar al-Kindi in the ninth century[2], and in Europe it gained a second use when movable type arrived around 1450: a printer had to estimate how much type to cast for each letter, and the different sizes of the compartments in a type case still show the proportions they chose. Edgar Allan Poe used the idea to solve the cipher in his story “The Gold-Bug”, and a cryptography text by Herbert Zim gives the sequence “ETAON RISHD LFCMU GYPWB VKJXZQ”. Today the same frequencies help in puzzle games from hangman to Wheel of Fortune and, of course, Wordle.

The twist

A good opening guess in Letter Lock is made from the letters that rank high in this list. Ours runs E, R, A, T, O, S, I, L, N, C; the running-text order puts H and D in its top ten and our list does not. The right frequency table is the one for your own list, which is why we counted it.
