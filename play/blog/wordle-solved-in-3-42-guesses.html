Wordle has been solved: 3.42 guesses on average, starting with SALET

Two operations researchers worked out the perfect Wordle strategy. Being perfect still takes more than three guesses.

Word games · The Ziggle team · 5-minute read · 15 October 2026

In this article 6 sections

Wordle gives you six guesses and a list of 2,315 possible answers[2]. In 2022, Dimitris Bertsimas and Alex Paskov of MIT asked a cleaner question than “what is a good opening word?”: what is the best possible strategy, provably? They modelled the game as a Markov decision process (a standard way to describe step-by-step decisions with uncertain outcomes) and solved it exactly. The paper was published in the journal Operations Research in 2025.

What perfect looks like

  • SALETthe best opening word
  • 3.42guesses on average with perfect play
  • 2,315answers in the original list
  • 6guesses allowed

Perfect play averages 3.42 guesses[1]. So if you solve a Wordle in four, you are within a guess of the theoretical best, and a three is a very good day. A fair share of the work is done by the first word: SALET is the best opening word the authors found[1].

An unexpected word

SALET is not a word most players would pick; it is a variant spelling of sallet, a light medieval helmet. The optimiser does not care about familiarity. It cares about how evenly a guess splits the remaining possibilities, and a word full of the most useful consonants and vowels, in the most telling positions, splits them well. That is the same idea as the information measure we use in the maths of a good guess.

The twist

The perfect strategy is not a list of words to memorise. It is a decision tree: after each pattern of colours, one best next guess. People cannot hold the tree in their heads, which is rather the point of a puzzle. You are meant to reason, not recite.

Our own game has the same structure with a different list: Letter Lock has 809 answers and 5,049 allowed guesses. We worked out its best opener from the lists themselves.

The deeper read

That was the short version. What follows goes further, with each fact read in its source.

How much a guess can possibly tell you

Each of the five tiles can come back in one of three colours, so a guess can produce at most 35 = 243 different patterns. Counting information in bits, the answer list of 2,315 words needs about 11.2 bits to single out one word, and a guess that split the words as evenly as possible across all 243 patterns would reveal at most 7.9 bits (both computed). One guess therefore cannot be enough to name the answer, since 11.2 is more than 7.9; two guesses could in principle, and then the answer has to be typed in as a third, unless luck gives it away sooner. Seen that way, a perfect average of 3.42 is close to the floor. Real guesses never split the words perfectly evenly across the patterns, so they reveal less than the maximum, and that is the gap the optimal strategy squeezes.

Where the game came from

The game began as a private present. Josh Wardle built a version of it for himself and his partner, publishing it on the web for just the two of them in January 2021, and he named it as a pun on his own surname. It was released to everyone in October 2021. What changed it was a small feature: a shareable grid of coloured squares that showed how you got on without spoiling the answer, which spread across social media. In January 2022 The New York Times bought it for what was described as a “low seven-figure sum”[3].

  1. January 2021Wardle publishes the game on the web, shared only with his partner and himself.
  2. October 2021Wordle is released to the public.
  3. Late 2021The emoji-grid result sharing makes it viral.
  4. January 2022The New York Times buys it for “a low seven-figure sum”; it stays free.
  5. July 2022Seven words have been removed from the original 2,315, so the Times version no longer matches older cached copies.
  6. November 2022Tracy Bennett becomes the first editor[3], choosing each day’s word from a curated list.

A list that is edited, and what that does to “perfect”

The optimal strategy was computed for the original list of 2,315 answers. The Times then began to curate it. Bennett removes words with a second, offensive meaning and those that might hurt, and some British spellings such as “fibre” were taken out. The words most complained about, she noted, were the unfamiliar ones: PARER, RUPEE, and the American-only CONDO. Some days now carry a theme: BEGIN on her first day, MEDAL on Veterans Day, FEAST on Thanksgiving.

For a solver, the lesson is that “perfect” belongs to a particular list. A strategy that is optimal for 2,315 words is only close to optimal for 2,308 or for a list that picks answers with human care. It is still a very strong guide, but it is a bit like a map of a city whose streets are renamed every few months.

The twist

Wordle’s player count peaked in March 2022. A year later it was about half of that peak[3] and, according to the Times, stable there. A free puzzle that takes a few minutes, has a single answer a day, and can be shared without spoiling it, turned out to be a habit-forming shape; it is the same shape our daily set aims for.
