How heavy is a web page? A median phone page is 18 Ziggle pages

Every page here has a weight limit. Here is what it is, and what the rest of the web weighs.

Behind Ziggle · The Ziggle team · 5-minute read · 9 November 2026

In this article 6 sections
  1. Where the weight goes
  2. Our rule
  3. What a smaller page buys you

Websites have got heavier every year. The HTTP Archive, which measures millions of pages every month, reported in its 2024 Web Almanac that the median page weighs 2,652 KB on a desktop[1] and 2,311 KB on a phone[1] (October 2024 data). That is more than the entire text of several novels, to load a page you may read for ten seconds.

Where the weight goes

The JavaScript on a typical phone page (570 KB) is about 4.4 times our whole page limit.

Our rule

Every Ziggle page has a budget: the files it needs up front, compressed, must come to no more than 130 KB, and a test fails the build if one goes over. The median mobile page is about 18 times heavier than that limit. A page that small loads quickly on a poor connection, uses little data, and, because we load nothing from other sites, has nothing else hiding in it.

The twist

A budget is a design tool, not a restriction. Because every new feature has to fit, we choose what is worth its bytes: a sound that makes a game feel good, or a how-to box that is only loaded when it is opened. The how-to dialog’s own styles load on demand for exactly this reason. Constraints keep you honest about what you really need.

What a smaller page buys you

Lighter pages are not only for people on slow connections. They use less battery, finish loading before you lose interest, and keep working offline once installed. The weight limit is the reason Ziggle can run as an installable app and still start before you have finished tapping the icon.

The deeper read

That was the short version. What follows goes further, with each fact read in its source.

A median phone page, in pieces

The Web Almanac breaks the median mobile homepage down by what it is made of:

A median mobile page also makes 66 requests[1] (71 on desktop), including about 22 separate JavaScript files. In 2024 JavaScript overtook images as the most requested kind of file. A Ziggle game page, by comparison, is a handful of files of our own, from our own site, and nothing else.

Why the Almanac calls it an accessibility issue

The chapter opens by saying that page weight is an accessibility issue: every extra kilobyte costs more for people on slow connections, small data plans or older phones, and it competes with the computing the rest of the internet needs. Its authors also point to the rising demand on data centres. A light page is a courtesy to the reader and a small saving for everyone else.

The twist

The median mobile page carries about as many bytes of fonts (111 KB) as most of our pages do of everything: our whole budget for one page, scripts, styles, pictures and all, is 130 KB. We load no web fonts and no third-party scripts at all, which is why a Ziggle page can load before the font on a typical news site has arrived.
