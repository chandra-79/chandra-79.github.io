The best first guess in Letter Lock (we tried all 5,049)

A good first guess removes nearly everything. A bad one barely scratches the list. Here is the data from our own word lists.

Word games · The Ziggle team · 5-minute read · 16 May 2026

Letter Lock picks one of 809 words and gives you six guesses. Before you type anything, the game could be any of them, which is 9.66 bits of uncertainty (log base 2 of 809). Each guess returns one of at most 35 = 243 colour patterns, so one guess can supply up to about 7.9 bits, never more.

We tried every one of the 5,049 allowed guesses against every one of the 809 answers and measured two things: the information the pattern provides on average, and how many answers are still possible afterwards.

The results

The winner by information, out of all 5,049 allowed guesses, is CRATE: about 6.03 bits, leaving around 19 answers on average from 809, so you have thrown out 97.7% of the list in one move.

Why ADIEU is worse than you would think

ADIEU is a popular opener because it has four vowels. It leaves about 44 answers, twice as many as CRATE, because vowels are common in nearly every answer, so their colours tell you less than the position of a consonant such as T or R. The best guesses mix the commonest consonants and vowels in the positions where they split the list most evenly.

The twist

CRATE is not the best opener because it is likely to be the answer; it is the best because of how it splits the list. The same idea, carried out exactly for Wordle’s list, gave the opener SALET: see Wordle has been solved.

After every game, Letter Lock’s coach shows how many answers were still in play after each of your guesses. You can see for yourself how your openers compare.

The deeper read

That was the short version. What follows goes further, with each fact read in its source.

The top ten, out of all 5,049

Every one of the ten contains both A and E, 9 contain R and 7 contain T (computed). Our reading: the best openers are built from the commonest letters, placed so that their colours split the list evenly.

Where the famous openers rank

  • 4.45median bits across all 5,049 guesses
  • 112guesses worth at least 5.5 bits
  • 8guesses worth at least 5.9 bits
  • 1.91the worst guess (YUKKY)

Computed across every allowed guess: half give 4.45 bits or less, the top tenth give more than 5.17, and only 858 give 5 or more. The best guess is worth about 1.6 bits more than the median guess, which shrinks the list about three times as much (our arithmetic: 21.6 ≈ 3).

What one guess does to the list

The biggest group is the all-grey one: 53 answers (6.6%), such as IMPLY, BLINK, UNION, SHOWN, SPOOL, ONION. A bad outcome from CRATE is still a good outcome in the sense that matters: the worst case leaves 53 of 809 words, and the average leaves 18.7.

Every pattern, and the five that never happen

Each guess can in principle return any of 243 colour patterns (35). Across all 4,084,641 guess-and-answer pairs, 238 of those patterns actually turn up. The five that never do are the ones with four greens and one yellow, because if four letters are in the right place the fifth cannot be somewhere else in the word (computed, and checked by the build). The all-grey pattern is the most common: 20.7% of all pairs.

What the best opener does for the whole game

This is not what a person does, and it assumes a perfect memory of 5,049 words, but it sets a ceiling: on this list with this opener, no answer needs more than four guesses from this program.

The twist

The opener that tells you the most is a word that cannot be the answer. CRATE is not one of the 809 words in the game. The best opener that can win outright, TRACE, is no more informative and leaves a few more answers standing. A guess that rules out the most beats a guess that might be right.
