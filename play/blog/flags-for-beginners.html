Square, double-pennant and everything in between: a flag primer

Only two national flags are square, one isn’t a rectangle at all, and flag fans have a five-rule test for good design.

Flags look simple. A few colours, maybe a shape or two. But spend ten minutes with people who study them and you’ll learn that flags are packed with rules, exceptions and arguments, which is exactly what makes them fun to learn.

A word for flag fans

The study of flags is called vexillology. The American scholar Whitney Smith came up with the term in 1957, and it first appeared in print in 1959. It comes from the Latin vexillum, a square flag carried by Roman cavalry, plus the Greek -logia, “study of”.

The rule-breakers

  • Nepal has the only national flag that isn’t a rectangle. It’s two stacked pennants, it’s taller than it is wide, and the constitution includes step-by-step geometric instructions for drawing it. Its height-to-width ratio is an irrational number, which is the most flag-nerd sentence we’ve ever written. The current design was adopted on 16 December 1962.
  • Switzerland and Vatican City have the only two square national flags. Switzerland’s square shape goes back to the war flags of its infantry.

What makes a good flag?

In 2006, Ted Kaye of the North American Vexillological Association wrote a short guide called “Good” Flag, “Bad” Flag. It boils good design down to five principles:

  1. Keep it simple, so a child could draw it from memory.
  2. Use meaningful symbolism.
  3. Use two or three basic colours that contrast well.
  4. No lettering or seals.
  5. Be distinctive, or be related to flags you belong with.

Not everyone agrees. One critic points out that “simple” and “distinctive” can pull in opposite directions: the simpler flags get, the more they start to look alike. Try drawing five tricolours from memory and you’ll see the problem.

Why emoji flags sometimes turn into letters

Here’s a fun technical twist. Country flag emoji are actually two special letters stuck together, taken from the country’s two-letter code. U plus S makes the US flag. Whether you see a flag or just the letters depends on your device and its fonts: a system that does not draw flags shows the two letters instead. England, Scotland and Wales use a different, newer method called tag sequences, approved in 2017.

That’s why Ziggle’s flags learner draws its own flags rather than relying on emoji. A flag quiz that shows the answer “US” in big letters wouldn’t be much of a quiz.

The deeper read

That was the short version. What follows goes further, with each fact read in its source.

Who named the study of flags, and when

Wikipedia’s article on vexillology says[1] the American scholar Whitney Smith is acknowledged for conceiving the word in 1957, and quotes him that the term did not appear in print until 1959. Before that, flags were generally treated as a part of heraldry. In 1961 he formalised the field by publishing The Flag Bulletin, and he went on to organise the first International Congress of Vexillology, the North American Vexillological Association (NAVA) and the International Federation of Vexillological Associations (FIAV). The congress has met every two years since 1969, under FIAV. A person who studies flags is a vexillologist, one who designs them a vexillographer, and a hobbyist a vexillophile.

Nepal’s one-off shape, in numbers

The article is specific about why the ratio is irrational: the red field on its own has a perfectly tidy 3:4 ratio, and the number only becomes irrational when the blue border is added to it by the construction the constitution lays down. A flag you can build with a ruler and compass has a proportion nobody can write as a fraction.

The two square flags

Switzerland’s national flag is 1:1 and was adopted in 1840. Its civil and state ensign, the version used at sea, is a standard 2:3 rectangle, adopted in 1882. The arms of the white cross are rectangles in a ratio of 7:6. According to the article’s footnote, the square shape comes from late medieval and early modern war flags carried by infantry regiments, while most other national flags follow the rectangular maritime flags of the age of sail. Swiss cantons and communes use square flags too.

The only other square national flag is that of Vatican City: a vertical bicolour of yellow and white with the emblem of the Holy See centred on the white half, adopted on 7 June 1929 and most recently modified on 7 June 2023.

What shapes do the flags Ziggle draws have?

The common 3:2 is the shape you picture when you picture a flag. The odd ones are in the data on purpose: the longest flag in the set is Qatar at 28:11, and the nearest to square, after Switzerland, is Belgium at 15:13 (computed from the learner’s data).

The five rules, and who published them

The five design principles come from a booklet, “Good” Flag, “Bad” Flag, that Ted Kaye compiled for NAVA in 2006: keep the design simple enough for a child to draw from memory; use meaningful symbolism; limit colours to two or three that contrast well; avoid lettering or seals; make the flag distinctive, or related to the flags it shares a context with. Wikipedia notes it has been translated into French, Spanish, German, Italian and Portuguese.

How an emoji flag is spelled

The regional indicator symbols are a set of 26 characters, A to Z, intended to spell the two-letter ISO 3166-1 country codes in a way that lets software show a flag if it chooses to. They were defined by October 2010 as part of Unicode 6.0. Because a flag is two of them, there are 26 × 26 = 676 possible pairs (computed), though only real country codes make flags.

The same article says a regional indicator can be shown as a Roman letter or as a flag at the system’s choice, which is why a device without flag artwork shows the two letters.

The twist

Nepal’s flag is defined by a construction written into its constitution, and that construction yields a proportion no fraction can express, even though the red field inside it is a plain 3:4. The first of the five rules asks for a flag a child could draw from memory. The one flag with the strangest geometry is a flag you have to construct.
