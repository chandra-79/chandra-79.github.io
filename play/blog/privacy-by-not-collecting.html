The safest data is the data we never collect

Ziggle needs no account, sets no cookies and has no trackers. Here’s what that means in practice, and what it costs us.

Behind Ziggle · The Ziggle team · 4-minute read · 19 June 2026

Europe’s data protection law, the GDPR, includes a principle called data minimisation: collect only what you actually need for a stated purpose. It is a good rule even where the law doesn’t require it.

What Ziggle keeps, and where

  • Your progress lives in your browser. Streaks, puzzles, trainer levels: all saved on your device. A backup file lets you move them yourself.
  • No account needed. There is nothing you must sign up for. Syncing to another device uses a short code, stored only as a scrambled (hashed) value, and there is an optional email sign-in for people who want it.
  • No cookies, trackers or outside scripts. Pages load nothing from other sites. The security rules for the site actively block it.
  • No IP addresses stored. Leaderboard entries keep a name you choose (or none) and the result.
  • Kids mode sends nothing to the server at all.

What it costs

We can’t email you a reset link, because we don’t know who you are. If you clear your browser data without a backup, your progress goes with it, which is why Settings has a Progress tab on every page, with a one-click backup.

We think that trade is worth it. A puzzle site doesn’t need to know who you are to give you a good puzzle.

The deeper read

That was the short version. What follows goes further, with each fact read in its source.

An old principle with many names

The Wikipedia article defines data minimisation as collecting, processing and storing only the amount of personal information a specific purpose needs. Its reasoning is that processing data you do not need creates risk for the person it is about[1], from identity theft to unreliable inferences that lead to wrong or even dangerous decisions, without any present benefit. What Ziggle stores, and for how long

Every item in Ziggle’s privacy notice is an application of the same question: do we need this to do the thing the person asked for? Here is the server side, using the retention periods in the notice. Everything on this list is opt-in or has an off switch, and none of it is needed to play.

  • Leaderboard entry (opt-in): a random player ID, the public name you choose, and the result, such as time, hearts and hints. Ninety days.
  • Sync copy (opt-in): your backup file, stored under a SHA-256 hash of your sync code, so the code is not kept. One hundred and eighty days after you last use it.
  • Visit counts (on by default, with an off switch): day-level tallies only, such as “12 visits whose previous visit was yesterday”. They are never sent if your browser says Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, in kids mode, or after you turn them off.
  • Feedback: only the message, its topic and the page it came from, for 180 days. No name, email, player ID or IP address.
  • Email account (opt-in): a hash of the normalised address, a masked version, a copy of your progress and up to ten hashed device tokens. A weekly email is a separate opt-in, off by default.

An earlier version of this post said Ziggle has no accounts at all. That is no longer exact: there is now an optional email sign-in for people who want to move their progress between devices, described above. Nothing in the game needs it.

Smaller details that follow from the principle

IP addresses are used only in memory, to limit how fast one client can send requests, and are never written to disk. If a page hits an error, a short note with the message, page path and time stays in your own browser, keeps the last 20 and is never sent anywhere. Online requests that fail, such as a leaderboard result, can wait in your browser for up to two days, at most 20 of them, and kids mode pauses all retries. The privacy notice also says what we cannot promise: server snapshots, of which the newest seven daily ones are kept, copy the same data, so a deleted record can survive until it is rotated out; deleting an account does not erase backups you have downloaded; and the email provider has its own retention.

  • 0cookies, outside scripts and trackers
  • 0IP addresses written to disk
  • 7daily server snapshots kept, then rotated out

What the principle costs

The post lists the main costs and they are real: no way to send a reset link to someone we do not know, and progress that goes if browser data is cleared without a backup. There is a third, less visible. Without accounts, some things that players like cannot exist. We do not send streak reminders from a server. In the apps, reminders are local: they use your device’s notification permission and never tell the server what time you picked. We accept the trade because the other side of it is concrete: the data we never collect cannot be leaked from our server, and it never needs a long privacy policy to explain.

The twist

The article’s argument is about risk, not virtue: unneeded data creates risk with no current benefit. That is a calculation any small site can make for itself, and the cheapest data to protect is the data you never held.
